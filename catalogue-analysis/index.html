How we did this

Full transparency on sources, definitions and limits — nothing here is a black box.

Data source

Every published post on vitalcitynyc.org, pulled from the Ghost Content API and mirrored to data/catalogue.json; author bios and post counts from the Ghost authors endpoint (data/authors.json). This page reads two pre-aggregated files: data/catalogue_analysis.json (from analyze_catalogue.py) and data/subject_analysis.json (from analyze_subjects.py), both recomputed on each catalogue refresh. Counts reflect the pull stamped at top right.

Key definitions

  • Format is the content type assigned by the catalogue pipeline (opinion/commentary, policy, data analysis, podcast, Q&A, book review, map/tool, something else). The vocabulary mirrors the sections vitalcitynyc.org itself uses, and every call records the rule that produced it in a type_basis field. Full ordered rule set in methodology.md.
  • Beats are built from Ghost's topic tags, but not directly. The single topics field holds three different kinds of label: real subjects, issue section rubrics (table-of-contents headings like "Setting the Stage" that name no topic), and format labels that duplicate the type field. All 206 normalized tags are sorted by hand into those three buckets in analyze_subjects.py; the 135 subjects are then mapped to 21 beats. The rubric and format lists are written into the output file so the sort can be audited. Seven tags that exist twice in Ghost — once with a trailing period — are normalized together.
  • A piece counts toward every beat it touches, so beat counts sum to more than the number of pieces. Most pieces (about 6 in 10) carry two beats.
  • Contributor background is a single label per piece, applied to the piece's primary author. It is assigned by an automated reading of the author's bio: the first professional role named in the bio wins (bios are written "is a [main role] and…"), with a short hand-checked override list for the most prolific contributors. It is an approximate heuristic, not a hand-verified label for all 472 authors.

Limitations worth keeping in mind

  • 2021 and 2026 are partial years. Vital City launched in September 2021, and 2026 runs only through the pull date — so those bars are not comparable to full years. They are flagged in the chart.
  • Contributor background is single-label and heuristic. Many contributors hold several credentials at once — a former prosecutor who now teaches law, a reporter who runs a nonprofit. We assign the role the bio leads with. Roughly one in ten pieces lands in "Other / unclassified" (mostly one-time contributors whose bios don't state a clear role) or "Unknown / no bio."
  • The "Vital City (institutional)" slice is pieces bylined to the journal itself (staff-written explainers, surveys, round-ups), not a person.
  • Beats inherit the tagging. Topic tags are applied by editors over five years and are not perfectly consistent; a piece that should carry a subject tag and doesn't will be missing from that beat. 17 pieces carry no subject tag at all (mostly press releases, event notices and a few pieces tagged only with a rubric) and are excluded from beat counts; they are listed in the output file.
  • The beat map is a judgment, and a contestable one. Sorting "Corruption" under courts rather than city government, or "Infrastructure" under transportation rather than the built city, moves real numbers. The mapping is one readable dictionary in analyze_subjects.py — disagree with a call and you can change it and re-run.
  • Trends are shares, not counts. Because output grew every year, a beat can fall as a share while holding steady in absolute terms — which is exactly what happened to criminal justice. Tagging density is stable across the period (2.1–2.4 subject tags per piece each year), so the shifts reflect coverage rather than changing tagging habits.

Reproducibility

Two stdlib-only scripts produce every number on this page: analyze_catalogue.py (volume, format, credentials, length) and analyze_subjects.py (beats, trends, co-occurrence). Both read the catalogue JSON and write their aggregates to disk. The credential keyword rules, the topic-to-beat map and the rubric and format lists all live in those files as readable dictionaries, commented. analyze_subjects.py fails loudly if a topic tag is not sorted into one of its three buckets, so a newly added tag cannot be silently dropped.
